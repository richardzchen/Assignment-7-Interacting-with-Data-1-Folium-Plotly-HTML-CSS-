Population, Community Belonging And Mental Health

1. Indigenous Population Composition

Open Map In A New Tab ↗

Select a layer to compare total Indigenous counts or First Nations, Inuit, and Métis shares of each province or territory’s total population. Hover over a region for exact counts and percentages.

Each layer uses its own six quantile classes. Compare exact values in the tooltips; colors are not a common numerical scale across layers.

2. Community Belonging And Mental Health Support

Open Scatterplot In A New Tab ↗

Use Play or the sex slider to switch between Both Sexes, Males, and Females. Hover for each location’s percentages and survey denominators. Population groups use distinct colors and symbols.

Axes remain fixed at 0–100%. Missing percentages are omitted. Only one Inuit record, from Nunavut, is available per frame. On narrow screens, scroll horizontally within the chart or open it in a new tab.

3. Composition Of The Three Largest Indigenous Populations

Open Donut Charts In A New Tab ↗

Ontario, Quebec, and Alberta are ordered by their total Indigenous population. Each donut shows First Nations, Inuit, and Métis shares within that location’s Indigenous population only. Hover for counts and exact percentages.

First Nations is the largest group in all three. Quebec has the largest First Nations and Inuit shares, while Alberta has the largest Métis share. On narrow screens, scroll horizontally to view all three donuts.

Source: the supplied 2021 Canadian population and health-inequality data and Canada GeoJSON. First Nations in the scatterplot refers to First Nations off reserve. All interactive HTML files are preserved.
